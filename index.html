Seriously, How?
How the hidden systems of modern life actually work.

The internet, the power grid, GPS, the planet itself: modern life runs on systems you never see, until they fail. Seriously, How? (on YouTube as @SeriouslyHowTV) makes cinematic, carefully sourced science documentaries about how they work, what happens when they break, and the people and physics holding them together. Latest documentary

On 27 October 2025, Hurricane Melissa was a Category 5 storm south of Jamaica, and a US Air Force Reserve crew, the 53rd Weather Reconnaissance Squadron or "Hurricane Hunters", flew straight into it: through the eyewall and into the calm eye. The film follows them and the numbers they brought back, from a dropsonde that measured 191-knot winds a few hundred metres above the sea to the 908-millibar reading at the centre, public eleven minutes after it hit the water. Footage from inside the storm was filmed on Melissa missions (U.S. Air Force, via DVIDS); satellite images are NOAA GOES-19 data.

Airport barometers, tide gauges and a nuclear-test listening post in Hawaii all recorded it. The same eruption cut Tonga's only international internet cable. We rebuilt the day from real data. How we work

Every number on screen is checked against a primary source and listed in the video description: network operators, regulators, standards bodies and the companies' own post-mortems. When something can't be verified, we leave it out or say it's an estimate. The narration is a synthetic voice; the research, scripts and fact-checks are done and reviewed by people.
