What still works without the internet?

If the internet stopped tomorrow, a surprising amount would keep running. Not because it's magic, but because some of the most important systems were deliberately built on separate networks.

Phone calls and texts (mostly)

Most voice calls and SMS travel across mobile and fixed carriers' own networks, and between carriers over private interconnects such as the GSMA IPX, a private IP network separate from the public internet. So an ordinary call or text would usually still go through. What stops: Wi-Fi calling and app-based calls and messages. Two caveats: the carrier's own core can fail (that's what happened to Rogers in 2022), and if millions switch to ordinary calls at once, networks can congest.

GPS

Your position comes from satellites, which don't need the internet. Without mobile data your phone lacks "assistance" data, so the first fix can take a few minutes instead of seconds. Online maps won't load, but maps you downloaded for offline use keep working.

The power grid

Grid control systems are required to sit behind their own electronic security perimeters (in North America, under the NERC CIP-005 standard). That doesn't mean they are fully "air-gapped", but they don't depend on the public internet to keep the lights on.

Air traffic control

Planes would keep flying safely. In the US, the FAA's Telecommunications Infrastructure is a private network linking more than 4,000 sites. What would fail is everything around the flight: check-in, crew scheduling and bookings, as the July 2024 CrowdStrike incident showed when airlines cancelled more than 5,000 flights in a day.

Emergency calls and hospitals

Emergency calls travel over phone networks and dedicated emergency networks (such as ESInet in North America). Hospitals have rehearsed downtime procedures (paper forms, printed lists, phone calls), and they used them during the 2024 CrowdStrike outage.

Broadcast radio and TV

The oldest networks become the most important. Radio and television don't need the internet to reach you, which is why a battery-powered radio is still on every emergency checklist.

What doesn't work

Anything that depends on a server somewhere else: cloud apps, messaging, streaming, many smart-home features, and, in many countries, card payments, because terminals are configured to get online approval for every transaction. A little cash goes a long way.

Frequently asked questions

Do phone calls work without the internet?

Mostly yes. Carriers exchange voice and SMS traffic over their own networks and private interconnects (such as the GSMA IPX), not the public internet. Wi-Fi calling and app-based calls would stop, and a carrier's own core can still fail, as Rogers showed in 2022.

Does GPS work without internet?

Yes. GPS positioning comes from satellites. Without mobile data the first fix can take a few minutes because assistance data is missing, and online maps won't load, but downloaded offline maps keep working.

Would the power stay on if the internet went down?

The grid's control systems sit behind their own electronic security perimeters under NERC CIP-005, rather than depending on the public internet, so the lights would be expected to stay on.

Can you call emergency services without the internet?

Yes. Emergency calls travel over phone networks and dedicated emergency networks such as ESInet, not the public internet.
