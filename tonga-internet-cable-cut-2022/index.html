How long was Tonga without internet after the 2022 eruption?

On 15 January 2022 the Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai volcano erupted under the sea, and Tonga lost its full connection to the internet for 38 days. The break happened on the seafloor: a fast underwater flow set off by the eruption snapped the cable between Tonga and Fiji.

Short answer: 38 days. The eruption set off an underwater flow that ran for more than 100 km and broke the Tonga–Fiji submarine cable. Full service returned on 22 February 2022, after the repair ship Reliance replaced 92 km of the 827 km cable.

What happened on 15 January 2022

Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai lies about 65 km north of Nukuʻalofa, Tonga's capital. Activity resumed in December 2021, a large eruption followed on 14 January, and the climactic explosion came at about 04:14–04:15 UTC on 15 January 2022, which was 17:14 local time in Tonga (Matoza et al., Science, 2022).

The explosion did several things at once. It sent a pressure wave around the planet, which we cover in our article on the pressure wave that circled Earth four times. It pushed tsunami waves across the Pacific, some of which arrived earlier than a normal tsunami could. And under the sea, it set off the flow that cut Tonga off.

Why Tonga lost its internet

Tonga's international internet traffic ran through a submarine cable to Fiji, laid on the seafloor. The eruption set off a fast underwater flow that ran for more than 100 km (Clare et al., Science, 2023). The flow broke the cable. With the cable broken, Tonga was without a full internet connection.

The fastest underwater flow ever measured

The broken cable turned out to be a measuring instrument. Because scientists knew when the cable broke, they could use the timing of the breaks to work out how fast the flow was moving. The answer was up to 122 km/h, the fastest underwater flow ever measured (Clare et al., Science 381, 2023).

38 days to reconnect

The repair ship Reliance replaced 92 km of the 827 km Tonga–Fiji cable. Full service returned on 22 February 2022, 38 days after the eruption, according to the Cloudflare Radar blog and RNZ, both on that day.

What we leave out

This page does not describe how people in Tonga communicated during those 38 days, or what the repair cost. Those details are not in the sources we checked for our documentary, so we do not state them here.
